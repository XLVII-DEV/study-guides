<h1>Cellular Respiration: How Cells Harvest ATP</h1>
<p>Glucose stores energy in high-energy C–H bonds, but burning it all at once would cook the cell. Cellular respiration harvests electrons gradually via <code>NADH</code> and <code>FADH₂</code> across three stages to mint usable <code>ATP</code>.</p>
<div class="summary-strip">
  <div class="pill-box"><span class="pill-label">Overall Formula</span><code>C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + ~32 ATP</code></div>
  <div class="pill-box"><span class="pill-label">Final Electron Acceptor</span><strong>O₂</strong> (forming H₂O)</div>
  <div class="pill-box"><span class="pill-label">Anaerobic Backup</span><strong>Fermentation regenerates NAD⁺</strong></div>
</div>

  <h2 id="h-stages">Three Stages &amp; Where Each Runs</h2>
  <p>Location matters on every biology exam: eukaryotic cells split glycolysis in the cytosol from mitochondrial oxidation so oxygen can drive the final proton gradient.</p>

    </svg>
    <figcaption>Only Glycolysis happens outside the mitochondrion and requires zero O₂.</figcaption>
  </figure>
  <ol class="step-trace">
    <li><strong>Glycolysis (Cytosol)</strong>: invests 2 ATP to split 6-carbon glucose into two 3-carbon <strong>pyruvate</strong> molecules, netting 2 ATP and 2 NADH.</li>
    <li><strong>Pyruvate Oxidation + Citric Acid Cycle (Matrix)</strong>: strips CO₂ to form Acetyl-CoA, generating 2 GTP/ATP, 6 NADH, and 2 FADH₂ per glucose.</li>
    <li><strong>Oxidative Phosphorylation (Cristae)</strong>: electrons pump H⁺ into the intermembrane space; <strong>ATP synthase</strong> turns proton-motive force into ~28 ATP as <strong>O₂</strong> accepts electrons.</li>
  </ol>
  <div class="compare-grid">
    <div class="compare-box bad">
      <strong>Common Mistake</strong>
      Thinking fermentation stops because muscles run out of ATP directly. Without O₂, the Electron Transport Chain backs up and starves cells of empty <code>NAD⁺</code>.
    </div>
    <div class="compare-box good">
      <strong>Right Way</strong>
      Fermentation exists solely to oxidize <code>NADH → NAD⁺</code> so glycolysis can keep yielding 2 substrate-level ATP without oxygen.
    </div>
  </div>
  <details class="oral-check">
    <summary>Active Recall: What happens to ATP yield and pH inside the intermembrane space if DNP pokes proton holes in the inner mitochondrial membrane?</summary>
    <div class="oral-ans">Protons leak back into the matrix without passing through ATP synthase, collapsing the H⁺ gradient (pH rises toward matrix pH), stopping oxidative ATP synthesis while electron transport speeds up and releases energy as heat.</div>
  </details>
  <div class="key-takeaway"><strong>Key takeaway:</strong> Oxygen never enters glycolysis or the Krebs cycle directly—it simply clears the Electron Transport Chain so <code>NADH</code> can unload electrons.</div>
</section>
